# LoRA under TP: what the constrained measurement says, and one hypothesis refuted

Previous sessions produced two "findings" here that were the same measurement
error twice: comparing values that are SUPPOSED to differ per rank. So this round
is built as a constraint rather than as a search --
`lora_tp_replicate_probe.py` refuses to compare anything whose TP-axis placement
is not `Replicate`, and now also records WHY each parameter was excluded, because
absence from a disagreement list otherwise reads as "clean" when it can mean
"never compared".

Run on the dense diagnostic flavor (`kimi_k3_mini_diag_4l_mla_lora`) at tp2:
MoE top-k is a discrete choice, so on a MoE model a cross-rank comparison
measures route divergence instead of correctness.

## The control, which decides whether LoRA is involved at all

| run | Replicate-on-TP grads checked | disagreeing |
|---|---|---|
| LoRA **off** (`kimi_k3_mini_diag_4l_mla`) | 40 | **0** |
| LoRA **on** (`..._lora`) | 26 | 11 |

Same probe, same code path, same seed. **LoRA is the trigger**, and the AttnRes
projections are not independently broken -- which matters beyond LoRA, because it
means the TP legs of the published parallelism matrices are not implicated.

## The previous framing was an artifact of what got compared

Recorded before: "`q_a_proj.lora_b`'s gradient is identical across ranks on layer
0 and differs on layers 1, 2, 3", read as a defect localized to layers 1-3.

With the exclusion reasons dumped, layer 0 shows only

    layers.0.self_attn.q_a_proj.base.weight    no_grad

and no `lora_a` / `lora_b` entry at all. Layer 0 has **no `q_a_proj` adapter**, so
it was never compared. It is not clean; it is absent. (Nor does
`q_a_proj.lora_a` exist in *any* layer, unlike every other target, which have
both -- worth its own look.)

The accurate statement is stronger and simpler. Of the 68 LoRA parameters in this
model, exactly **three are Replicate on the TP axis -- the three
`q_a_proj.lora_b` -- and all three disagree.** 64 are Shard or Partial and are
correctly outside this test's scope. So the failure rate among LoRA gradients this
test can judge is 3 of 3, not "3 of many".

The other 8 disagreements are the AttnRes projections (`attn_res_proj`,
`mlp_res_proj`, `final_attn_res_proj`), which stay full-param trainable under
base-freeze by the alpha-fullparam exception, are Replicate, and sit downstream.
Note `layers.0.mlp_res_proj` disagrees at 5.16e-02 against a reference magnitude
of 4.58e-02 -- over 100% relative, and the earliest disagreement in the network.

## Hypothesis tested and refuted: a Partial intermediate

`parallelize.py` distributes a Rowwise-styled adapter as `lora_a = Shard(1)`,
`lora_b = Replicate()`. In `KimiLoRALinear.forward` the adapter is then

    lora_out = F.linear(F.linear(x, la), lb)

With `x` sharded on the contracted axis and `la` sharded on dim 1, the inner
result is **Partial**. Matmul is linear in it, so DTensor may legally carry the
Partial through the second matmul and reduce once at the end -- the forward is
right either way. The proposed defect: `lora_b`'s gradient is
`grad_out^T @ inter`, so each rank builds its own term of the sum and the result
comes out labelled `Replicate` while holding a partial value, with nothing
downstream reducing it because the label says there is nothing to reduce.

Tested by forcing the intermediate to `Replicate` before the second matmul.
**Refuted:** the three `q_a_proj.lora_b` deltas came back bit-identical
(4.262e-04, 1.951e-04, 1.627e-04), so that branch never fires for `q_a_proj` --
its intermediate is not Partial. The change also relabelled other adapters'
gradients from excluded to `Replicate`, exposing `o_proj.lora_b` and
`down_proj.lora_b` as disagreeing too, taking the count from 11 to 19. So it made
the measurement broader and the model no better. Reverted; not committed.

What that rules out: the Rowwise `lora_a`/`lora_b` composition inside `forward`
is not where `q_a_proj`'s gradient goes wrong.

## The authoritative result, and two of my own corrections withdrawn

Everything between here and the end of this section supersedes the two
"corrections" below it. Both were the same probe bug, described at the end.

**Step 1 understates the problem, because half the set is vacuously zero.**
`lora_b` is zero-init, so at step 0 nothing flows into `lora_a` and its gradient
is exactly zero on every rank; likewise every AttnRes `*_res_norm` weight. Zero
agrees with zero, and 14 of step 1's 15 "agreements" have
`ref_absmax = 0.000e+00`. They were never testable.

Running three steps, so those become non-zero:

| step | Replicate grads compared | non-zero (testable) | differ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 26 | 12 | 11 |
| 2 | 26 | 24 | **22** |
| 3 | 26 | 24 | **22** |

**22 of 24 testable Replicate-on-TP gradients disagree across ranks.** The only
two that agree, at any step, are `layers.0.self_attn.q_a_proj.lora_a`
(3.80e-05) and `.lora_b` (2.07e-03).

So the defect is near-total, not localized. Every `*_res_proj` with a non-zero
gradient differs, at every layer plus the final aggregation; every
`q_a_proj.lora_b` differs except layer 0's.

**The original handoff framing was right and my correction was wrong.** Layer 0's
`q_a_proj.lora_b` is genuinely clean -- non-zero (2.33e-03 at step 1, larger than
layers 1-3's by 5-10x) and identical across ranks. It is not "absent" and it is
not vacuous.

### The hypothesis this supports

Layer 0's `q_a_proj` is the one Replicate parameter upstream of every TP
collective: it consumes the embedding output, which no TP boundary has touched
yet. Everything else sits downstream of at least one. That shape -- clean at the
source, contaminated everywhere after the first boundary -- says the contamination
enters at a TP module boundary and is inherited, rather than being produced
independently in each adapter.

And LoRA-off is clean (40/40), so the wrapper is what breaks the boundary. The
mechanism to test: `parallelize.py` redirects a Colwise/Rowwise style from the
`KimiLoRALinear` onto its inner `.base`, so the style's input/output layout
conversion happens at the inner Linear's boundary. The adapter branch runs
OUTSIDE that boundary. The wrapper then adds a base output that went through the
conversion to an adapter output that did not.

Test, one measurement: for a single Colwise-styled LoRA module, record the
placements of `base_out` and `lora_out` immediately before they are summed. If
they differ in a way the sum silently coerces, that is the boundary.

### Why the two sections below are wrong

`compare_replicated_grads` originally wrote a record only when a gradient
DISAGREED. So a compared-and-agreeing parameter produced no output, and absence
meant "never compared" in one place and "compared and agreed" in another -- the
exact ambiguity this probe was written to remove, reintroduced at the other end.
145 parameters enumerated, 130 records, and 26 - 11 = 15 silent agreements
accounts for the gap exactly.

That is what made `q_a_proj.lora_a` look absent and layer 0 look adapter-less. The
probe now records agreements explicitly, with `ref_absmax` so a zero-gradient
agreement cannot be mistaken for a meaningful one.

Third instance of the same failure mode in this investigation: concluding from a
comparison between two things that were not comparable. The first two were
placements; this one was presence.

## Withdrawn: five parameters disappear during step 1

"`q_a_proj.lora_a` does not exist" was wrong, and so was "layer 0 has no
adapter". Dumping the structure right after `Trainer.__init__` shows all four
layers **structurally identical** and fully replicated:

    layers.N.self_attn.q_a_proj        KimiLoRALinear   children=['base']
          lora_a   [8, 512]    rg=True   ['R']
          lora_b   [128, 8]    rg=True   ['R']
    layers.N.self_attn.q_a_proj.base   Linear
          weight   [128, 512]  rg=False  ['R']

Everything about `q_a_proj` is Replicate -- it gets no Colwise/Rowwise style at
all, which independently confirms the refutation above.

Dumping the structure at init and the parameter set after `train_step` **in the
same process** gives the actual finding:

    LOST between init and step 1:
      layers.0.self_attn.q_a_proj.lora_a
      layers.0.self_attn.q_a_proj.lora_b
      layers.1.self_attn.q_a_proj.lora_a
      layers.2.self_attn.q_a_proj.lora_a
      layers.3.self_attn.q_a_proj.lora_a

Five parameters that are registered `nn.Parameter`s after `__init__` are absent
from `named_parameters()` by the end of the first step. Not a dedup artifact --
`remove_duplicate=False` gives the same set. Not a cross-run difference -- this is
one invocation.

A parameter leaves `named_parameters()` only if its attribute is rebound to
something that is not a `Parameter`. Every assignment to these attributes in our
tree wraps in `nn.Parameter` (4 sites: `lora.py` 134/137, `parallelize.py`
965/969), so the rebinding is happening somewhere else.

**This reframes the defect.** A parameter outside `named_parameters()` is also
outside the optimizer, so those adapters are **never updated** -- and
`layers.0.self_attn.q_a_proj` ends up with no trainable adapter at all. That is a
correctness bug that has nothing to do with TP, and it is a better candidate for
what the cross-rank disagreement on the survivors is downstream of.

It is also deterministic and bisectable, which the numerics were not: dump the
parameter set at four points -- after `__init__`, after the first forward, after
backward, after `optimizers.step()` -- and see which boundary loses them.

Throughout, compare only `Replicate`-on-TP values. Never compare plain per-rank or
Shard-placed locals.

## The boundary measurement (2026-08-06): hypothesis refuted, contradiction sharpened

Recorded the placements of `base_out` and `lora_out` immediately before they are
summed, for every LoRA module in layer 0, at tp2
(`/tmp/lora_boundary_probe.py`, pattern reusable):

| module | x | base_out | lora_out | lora_a | lora_b |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `q_a_proj` | R | R | R | R | R |
| `q_b_proj` | R | S(2) | S(2) | R | S(0) |
| `kv_a_proj_with_mqa` | R | R | R | R | R |
| `kv_b_proj` | R | S(2) | S(2) | R | S(0) |
| `attn_gate_proj` | R | plain | S(2) | R | S(0) |

**`q_a_proj` is fully replicated end to end** -- input, both adapter factors, base
output and adapter output all `Replicate`, and no Colwise/Rowwise style was
redirected onto its `.base` at all. So the hypothesis that the adapter branch runs
outside the style's layout conversion **is refuted for the one parameter whose
gradient disagrees**. The only mismatch in the table is `attn_gate_proj`, whose
plain `base_out` against a `Shard(2)` `lora_out` is the case the code's comment
anticipates and handles with `to_local()`.

### What that leaves, stated as a contradiction

For `q_a_proj`, all of the following are established:

* `x` is Replicate, so identical on both ranks.
* `lora_a` and `lora_b` are Replicate, so `inter = lora_a(x)` is identical.
* The gradient arriving at `q_a_proj`'s output is identical -- measured earlier via
  `q_a_layernorm`: its Partial output-grad differs per rank (0.000193 / 0.000178)
  while its Replicate input-grad is identical (0.000596 on both).

`lora_b.grad` is `grad_out^T @ inter`. With both factors identical on both ranks,
it cannot differ -- and it does, on layers 1-3 but not layer 0.

So exactly one of those three premises fails on layers 1-3 while holding on layer
0. That is now the whole question, and it is a comparison of two tensors, not a
search.

### Next measurement, and it is the last one this needs

On layer 1's `q_a_proj`, all-gather and compare across ranks, in this order:

1. `inter` (the adapter's intermediate activation), and
2. the gradient arriving at the module's output.

Whichever differs is the defect's entry point. If both are identical while
`lora_b.grad` differs, the problem is below our code -- a DTensor
Replicate x Replicate matmul backward producing a rank-dependent result -- and that
is a torch-level report, not a model fix.

Do NOT compare `q_b_proj`, `kv_b_proj` or `attn_gate_proj` gradients for this: they
are `Shard(0)`/`Shard(2)` and are supposed to differ across ranks.

## The premise that fails: the module INPUT differs, and this is not a LoRA defect

The comparison, layer 0 against layer 1, `q_a_proj`, tp2, one step:

| quantity | layer 0 | layer 1 |
|---|---|---|
| `x` placement | `Replicate` | `Replicate` |
| `base.weight` placement | `Replicate` | `Replicate` |
| `inter = lora_a(x)` | IDENTICAL | **DIFFERS, delta 1.096e+00 against magnitude 1.930e+00** |
| grad arriving at output | IDENTICAL | DIFFERS, 2.5e-07 against 2.3e-06 |
| `lora_b.grad` | IDENTICAL | (differs, per the table above) |

`lora_a` is Replicate, so `inter` can only differ if **`x` differs**. And `x` is
LABELLED Replicate. At 57% relative that is not rounding.

**So the defect is upstream of LoRA.** The residual stream entering layer 1 holds
rank-dependent values while its placement says Replicate -- the same failure shape
as everything else in this investigation, a Partial value wearing a Replicate
label. The obvious suspect is the attention output path: `o_proj` is Rowwise, so
its output is Partial and must be all-reduced to Replicate.

That also explains the control that looked clean. With LoRA off, 40 replicated
gradients agreed -- but in that region the trainable parameters are SHARDED, so
nothing was positioned to notice a replicated-but-unequal activation. LoRA did not
introduce this; `lora_b` is simply the first Replicate parameter whose gradient is
computed from that activation. Layer 0 is clean because its input is the embedding
output, which no TP collective has touched.

**One confirmation before acting on it**, because this investigation has twice
mistaken a legitimate difference for a defect: compare `x` itself across ranks
rather than inferring from `inter`, and dump the placement of layer 0's attention
output where it becomes layer 1's input. If `x` really is an unreduced Partial
labelled Replicate, the fix is in the attention output path and the scope is much
wider than LoRA -- every TP leg of the published matrices would be running with a
rank-divergent residual stream, which the loss reporting could mask.

Probes: `/tmp/lora_final_probe.py` (pattern; move into the repo before relying on
it -- /tmp has cost this project a matrix before).

## RESOLVED (2026-08-07): the Rowwise base bypass in KimiLoRALinear.forward

The confirmation the previous section asked for was run and it moved the site one
module upstream of where the reasoning had put it. Rather than compare `x` alone,
the whole residual stream of layers 0 and 1 was recorded in EXECUTION ORDER with
placements and cross-rank deltas -- because guessing between `o_proj`, `down_proj`
and the AttnRes bridges is what the measurement replaces.
Probe: `tp_residual_stream_probe.py` (in the repo; the earlier ones were in /tmp).

### The trace, tp2, `kimi_k3_mini_diag_4l_mla_lora`, one step

| seq | module | place | delta | mag | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | `layers.0.self_attn.q_a_proj` OUT | R | 0.000e+00 | 1.8e+00 | |
| 13 | `layers.0.self_attn.inner_attention` OUT | plain | 1.9e+00 | 1.1e+00 | correct: this rank's heads |
| 15 | `layers.0.self_attn.attn_gate_proj` OUT | plain | 2.7e+00 | 1.8e+00 | correct: Colwise local |
| **17** | **`layers.0.self_attn.o_proj` OUT** | **plain** | **3.457e-01** | **2.266e-01** | **must be all-reduced** |
| 20 | `layers.0.post_attention_layernorm` OUT | R | 5.250e+00 | 4.375e+00 | inherited |
| -- | `layers.0.ffn.down_proj` OUT | plain | 0.000e+00 | 2.8e-01 | same Rowwise style, CLEAN |
| -- | `layers.1.self_attn.q_a_proj` OUT | R | 1.285e+00 | 1.812e+00 | the symptom this began at |

With LoRA **off**, the identical trace on `kimi_k3_mini_diag_4l_mla`: `o_proj` OUT
`0.000e+00`, and every activation after it identical. **So the scope is LoRA + TP,
not TP.** The published matrices' non-LoRA TP legs are unaffected, and the earlier
worry that "every TP leg would be running with a rank-divergent residual stream"
does not hold.

### The defect

`KimiLoRALinear.forward` had a branch for "plain input but a DTensor base weight",
written for NoParallel descents (MoE shared experts, weight Replicate, where
`to_local()` is exact). It bypasses `self.base` entirely, so the registered style's
own collective never runs -- which is why no `o_proj.base` row appears in the trace
at all while every other target has one. With a **Rowwise** base the weight is
`Shard(1)`, the CONTRACTED axis, so the local product is only this rank's PARTIAL
contribution; `to_local()` drops the DTensor that would have summed it and the
partial value escapes as a plain tensor, which everything downstream reads as
replicated.

Why `o_proj` alone: it is the only Rowwise LoRA target reached with a plain input,
because MLA builds the attention output in plain-tensor land. The dense FFN's
`down_proj` is also Rowwise and also LoRA-wrapped, but receives a DTensor from the
Colwise gate/up pair, so `not x_is_dt` is false and it takes the `self.base(x)`
path. That asymmetry is exactly what the trace shows.

And it explains every earlier observation, including the ones that misdirected the
search: layer 0's `q_a_proj` was clean because it consumes the embedding output,
upstream of the first `o_proj`; the LoRA-off control was clean because the
bypass does not exist there; and the "contamination enters at a TP module boundary
and is inherited" shape was right about the shape and wrong about which boundary.

### Fix

`lora.py`: when the base weight is sharded on the input axis, lift the plain input
to `Shard(-1)`, keep the matmul in DTensor so it lands as Partial, and
`full_tensor()` it -- adding bias AFTER the reduction, or it is counted once per
rank. Colwise and Replicate weights keep the local path, where the local product
is already the right answer.
