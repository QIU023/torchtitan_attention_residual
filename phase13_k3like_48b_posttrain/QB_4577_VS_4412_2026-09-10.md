# Quantile balancing: Shuhua's #4577 against our #4412 (2026-09-10)

#4577 (`shuhuayu`, opened 2026-09-10, "alternative implementation to #4412", asks @QIU023 / @JavaZeroo to review) and #4412 (ours, head `895f4d6e9` on `65ba8a697`, 11 review comments). Both solve the report's Eq. 13-14 (sec. 2.3.3, App. D): the (k+1)-th biased score is the cutoff, the per-expert required bias `cutoff - score` is histogrammed, the `k/E` quantile becomes the mean-centred bias at the optimizer pre-hook. The maths agree (our `-quantile_{1-k/E}(score - cutoff)` is the same number); everything else differs.

## Where the two differ

| | #4412 (ours) | #4577 (Shuhua) | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| where the cutoff comes from | a `register_forward_hook` on the router recomputes `topk(scores + bias, k+1)` from the router's returned scores: a second top-k per forward (his comment on L221) | `QuantileBalancedTopKRouter(TokenChoiceTopKRouter)`: one `topk(k+1)` inside `forward`, first k route, the (k+1)-th is the cutoff, under `remat.region(..., recompute=False)` like core's router | ours is redundant work and can disagree with the router's own top-k on ties |
| histogram range | fixed `[-1, 1]`, 512 bins, margins outside clamped into the end bins, same for every layer | App. D: `[min(bias) - 1, max(bias) + 1]` from the current step's bias, 1000 bins, per layer | his comment on L164: ours is "inconsistent with the report"; with sigmoid scores the required bias lies exactly in his range, so his loses nothing to clipping |
| training vs eval forwards | the hook fires on every router forward, eval included (JavaZeroo's comment on L209) | `if not self.training: return super().forward(...)`; `observe` also checks `self.training` | ours is a bug: eval tokens land in the next training solve |
| wiring | `kimi_k3_debugmodel()` sets `model_spec.post_optimizer_build_fn = register_quantile_balancing` (debug flavor only); needs `load_balance_coeff` set so core creates `expert_bias_E`, then replaces core's hook | `model_registry(post_optimizer_build_fn=register_moe_quantile_balancing_hook)` for every flavor; `_latent_moe_config` builds the QB router with `num_bins=1000`; `load_balance_coeff=None`, and `KimiLatentMoE` owns a persistent `expert_bias_E` buffer of its own (raises if the sign rule is also asked for); the forward no longer accumulates `tokens_per_expert_E` | his comments on L66 / L290: it belongs in `model_registry`; our coupling to the sign-rule coefficient and the `tokens_per_expert_E` reset go away |
| reduction | one stacked all-reduce over the loss mesh; explicitly not over tp | per-layer async all-reduces over the loss mesh, plus the dense-tp mesh when EP is on and tp > 1 (the router is token-sharded on that axis under EP) | ours misses the tp ranks' tokens under EP x TP; his stacking-free loop is one collective per layer |
| state | a `QuantileBalancer` object outside the model, histograms in a dict keyed by `id(moe)`, `_armed` guard so a step without a forward does not solve | a `QuantileBalancer(Module)` owned by the router, histogram a non-persistent buffer re-created by `_init_self_buffers` (follows meta -> device), `expert_bias_E` persistent in the MoE | his follows titan's `Module` protocol; ours is a side structure the checkpointer and device moves do not see |
| solve | CDF normalised by the total, crossing bin interpolated by CDF fraction | target rank `ceil(counts * k/E)`, crossing bin by cumulative count, fraction by count in bin, `(position - mean) * bin_width` | equivalent; his divides by `counts_in_bin` with no guard, so a layer whose histogram is empty at a pre-hook (no forward since the last step, or an expert with zero mass in the crossing bin) yields NaN, which our `_armed` / `clamp(min=1e-12)` avoid |
| guards | none | sigmoid scores required, no group-limited routing, no `_debug_force_load_balance` | his |
| tests | 14 CPU tests (histogram vs exact solve, in-place zeroing, SAC-identical op sequence) + 10-step dp8 x ep8 / dp2 x ep2 tables | one 2-GPU `DTensorTestBase` test: router observation, the real all-reduce over dp_shard=2 x ep=2, the optimizer pre-hook, histogram reset | complementary: he exercises the collective, we exercise the estimator |

## What Shuhua's eight comments on #4412 amount to

Three nits (remove comments), "move the registration to `model_registry`" (L66, L290), the lambda spelling (L298), the fixed histogram range (L164), the second top-k (L221); plus JavaZeroo's eval-forward bug (L209). Every one of them is already the shape of #4577.

## What #4577 still lacks (for our review of it)

- The empty-histogram NaN above (a pre-hook with no forward since the last solve: step 0 after a checkpoint resume, or an MoE layer on a rank that saw no tokens); our `_armed` guard and the `counts_in_bin` clamp are the fix.
- One all-reduce per layer per step (92 collectives at full size) where one stacked `(L, E, B)` collective does it; our stacking is the fix.
- Under selective AC with a recompute of the router forward, `observe` runs twice for the recomputed micro-batch: harmless for the quantile (a uniform factor), but the int32 histogram fills twice as fast; worth a sentence in the docstring (ours has the measured note).
- Jeremalloch's point (Marin, Megatron keep it architecture-agnostic): the histogram / solve / hook belong in `models/common/moe.py` + `components/optimizer`, with only the router config and the `post_optimizer_build_fn` wiring in `kimi_k3`.

## What to do with #4412

The maintainers' implementation is the one that will merge; keep our numerics tables and the estimator tests as the review material, offer the three fixes above as review comments on #4577, and close #4412 once they land (or turn it into the numerics/CI PR on top of his). The K3 integration tree's QB (`k3_qb` = `895f4d6e9`) should be re-based on his router once #4577 merges: the flavor stops setting `post_optimizer_build_fn`, and the QB numerics rerun on the loss mesh + dense-tp reduction.
