# Alpha in FP32: matching the release, and what it bought

## The change

`block_attn_res` now computes the whole of alpha in FP32 and casts back once at
the end, matching the official `_apply_attn_res`. Previously it cast DOWN to the
stream dtype at two points -- after the norm and at the query -- to match FSDP
mixed precision.

    K = K.float()                                  # was: K.to(V.dtype)
    query = weight.squeeze(0).float()              # was: query.to(K.dtype)
    h = einsum("nbt,nbtd->btd", weights, V.float())
    return h.to(V.dtype)                           # single cast, at the end

The two load-bearing constraints are preserved: `norm(V)` still runs before the
`proj.weight` access (it is what all-gathers proj under FSDP2), and the DTensor
unwrap at the use-site is unchanged.

## What it bought

One dense MLA layer, pure TP, per-parameter grad-norm ratio dp1/tpN:

                    bf16 alpha    FP32 alpha
    max |ratio-1|      0.0018       0.00002   (tp2)
                       0.0018       0.00003   (tp4)

Sixty to ninety times better. That is the cancellation showing up: alpha's
projection is zero-initialized, the block softmax starts uniform, and the
pseudo-query gradient is a difference of nearly equal terms -- measured earlier
this session at 6x to 15x. Doing that arithmetic in bf16 was costing exactly
what the release's FP32 avoids.

## Matrix, single axes

    dp1     7.72376 7.14969 6.22981   unchanged
    pp2     7.66117 7.04581 6.12275   unchanged
    tp2     7.70027 7.04398 6.15859   unchanged
    fsdp2   7.67816 7.27396 6.28982   was 7.67856 7.26904 6.29599
    cp2     7.69580 7.21154 6.27141   was 7.69600 7.21146 6.26816

Only the FSDP-bearing legs move, which is the expected shape: those are the ones
whose mixed-precision cast the old code was matching. The rest are bit-identical.

## A false alarm worth recording

The first measurement after the change reported max |ratio-1| of 0.14 (tp2) and
0.40 (tp4) -- an apparent 200x regression. The seed checkpoint was built with the
OLD operator, so the run was measuring an operator against weights that did not
belong to it. Rebuilding the seed with the new operator gives the numbers above.

Any change to alpha invalidates seeds built before it.
