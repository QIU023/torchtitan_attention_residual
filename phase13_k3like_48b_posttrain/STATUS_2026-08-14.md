## 2. The AttnRes fp32 fix

`block_attn_res` normalized in the stream dtype and cast to fp32 after; the release floats
first, so variance and rsqrt are fp32 too. One token: `K = norm(V.float())`.

Full write-up in `ATTNRES_FP32_2026-08-14.md`. The three measurements that settle it:

| | float32 | bfloat16 |
|---|---|---|
| parity vs the vendored reference, before | 1.3e-07 | 1.9e-03 |
| parity vs the vendored reference, after | 1.3e-07 | **0.0** |
| gradients old vs new (unit level) | **bitwise identical** | proj.weight 2.5e-01 |

Three-arm gate, complete:

| arm | dtype | cells training | step 1 | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mm_full 13-cell | bf16 | 13/13, unchanged | bit-identical | PASS |
| mm_full maxdeg | bf16 | 5/5, unchanged | bit-identical | PASS |
| mm_lora 13-cell | bf16 | 13/13, unchanged | bit-identical | PASS |
| text 13-cell | fp32 | 10/13, unchanged (3 are the pre-existing SM120 ceiling) | bit-identical | drift not this change |

**Step 1 is bit-identical in all 44 cells**, and drift enters at step 2 everywhere. That is
what a defect in the zero-init pseudo-query's GRADIENT predicts: at step 1 the block softmax
is exactly uniform at any precision, so there is nothing to lose until `proj` has taken a
step. The matrix produced this; it was not a test written to find it.

Text arm (float32, where it cannot act): the ~1e-2 drift there is **not** this change. The
unit-level gradient comparison above is bitwise identical in fp32, and step 1 matches in
every cell. What is left between the two trees is migration step A, whose byte-identity was
only ever verified on the multimodal arm. The attribution control is queued.

## 3. The two core changes, refuted

Neither premise had ever been executed.

**`pipeline_parallel.py`'s divide.** Upstream is self-consistent in three places that
agree -- the dataloader is built at the microbatch size, `arg_mbs` is collected to
`local_batch_size // microbatch_size`, and `_build_pipeline_schedule` computes exactly that
from the undivided value. Our extra division is a no-op at the default microbatch size of 1
and wrong above it. It entered inside a commit titled "kimi_k3: compute alpha in FP32",
which is how it escaped review.

**`common/moe.py`'s scatter branch.** Measured on gloo/CPU, no model and no GPU: in-place
`scatter_` never raises and preserves `Shard(1)` on every placement combination the router
produces. The stated mechanism does not exist. The EP x TP failure it was written for was
fixed later and for a different reason -- the SP island.

Both reverted on `core_revert_probe`; the cells each one claimed to need are queued.

## 9. Method notes that earned their place today

* **A mechanism written down in detail is not a mechanism that was checked.** Both core
  changes carried a paragraph of justification and neither had been executed. The scatter
  one took four minutes to refute with a twenty-line gloo script.
* **Ask which trees differ before asking what the change did.** The text arm's FAIL looked
  like the change under test until the fp32 gradient comparison ruled it out in one CPU run.
* **A mechanical verdict has a criterion baked in.** `compare_to_dev_baseline.py` says FAIL
  on a 1e-4 threshold, which is a refactor gate. Applied to a deliberate computation change
  it is the wrong question, and both the FAIL and why it is the wrong question belong in
  the record.
* **Strengthen the probe before trusting its "before" number.** `attnres_parity.py` used a
  unit norm weight, which makes the query-folding arm vacuous; the pre-fix number was
  partly measured against nothing until that was fixed.
