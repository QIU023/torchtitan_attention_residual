This note records what the stack carries, what the probes found, and what the PR body can claim.

## The trap: "float32" that was bf16

`--training.dtype float32` on this tree gives float32 masters but bf16 compute: `parallelize` wraps dp1 in FSDP2 too, and `mixed_precision_param` defaults to bf16. The first three probe rounds compared bf16 forwards and found a 2.9e-3 gap in layer 0's `output_proj` that grew to 10 percent at the top of a 9-layer model and to gradient cosines of 0.8 to 0.95; it was two bf16 partial products summed against one bf16 matmul (both 1.7e-3 and 2.5e-3 from the fp64 product of the same dumped inputs). A probe is float32 only with `--training.mixed_precision_param float32 --training.mixed_precision_reduce float32` as well, and the trace prints the dtypes.

Everything after that inherits it. Under bf16 compute the same chain starts at 2.9e-3 and ends at 10 percent, which is what the bf16 tables show.

## The tower under spmd_types at tp > 1

4500 declares the tower's projector colwise / rowwise (its CP cells run at tp=1, where a Partial on the tp axis is trivially replicated). At tp=2 with sequence parallel off, the fp32 gradient dump showed every vision-encoder parameter's gradient exactly doubled against dp1 (relative difference 1.0 on all 70) and the rest of the model at 1e-2, while the SP-on cell was fine: the rowwise exit leaves a Partial that nothing between the tower and the splice reduces. The branch declares the tower invariant on tp under spmd_types at tp > 1 (the 4499 declaration: it runs whole on every rank, as under partial_dtensor). With the invariant tower the spmd_types SP-on cell reads the same step-1 loss and grad norm as partial_dtensor's (12.622550, 25.0 in the bf16 table), its float32 gradient distribution reproduces the control class by class, and the SP-off cell's float32 forward now matches dp1 at the same 3e-6 to 2.6e-5 per layer as partial_dtensor's (embedding output bitwise); the SP-off cell's gradients still sit at 1e-2 (loss-path probe below).

## What the PR body claims

The bar of the numerics rule: step-1 forward bitwise where the math is bitwise (everything up to the first sharded matmul), then every parameter inside the located floor. Here the located floor is the KDA recurrence's amplification of the sharded projection's fp32 roundoff; the bf16 five-step table sits next to the fp32 forward table and the gradient distribution.

## Why this box amplifies: the KDA kernel's precision, measured (2026-09-09, late)

The user asked why the same 4500 code spreads by percents here and by `0.15%` on the B200/H100 table. Not known yet; three measurements narrow it.

E2, the kernel (`matrix_scripts/tp_a100/kda_kernel_sensitivity.py`, attn-gym `chunk_kda`, `[1, 256, 16, 128]`, L2-normalized q/k, gates in `[-2, 0]`, no autotune):

| inputs | implementation | output change for a tiny input perturbation | same input twice | fused vs reference |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| fp32, perturbation `3e-7` | fused (Triton, the path torchtitan runs) | `2.2e-4` | bitwise | `4.5e-3` |
| fp32, perturbation `3e-7` | reference (eager fp32) | `5.5e-7` | bitwise | |
| bf16, perturbation `4e-3` | fused | `8.3e-3` | bitwise | `3.8e-3` |
| bf16, perturbation `4e-3` | reference | `7.8e-3` | bitwise | |

The delta rule itself passes a perturbation through at `1.8x` (reference); the fused kernel turns a `3e-7` change into `2.2e-4` and sits `4.5e-3` from the reference on fp32 inputs, the FP16-intermediate level its docstring names ("FP16 intermediates passed between FP32-accumulating GEMMs"). So every KDA layer carries a `4e-3`-class rounding source that no outer precision removes, which is why float32 masters and float32 compute did not close the 10-step spread and why the 3e-7 projection control moved gradients by `2e-4`. Whether the same kernel keeps FP16 intermediates on an A100 or H100, or whether the Triton path on SM120 is what stages them in FP16, is the A100 kit's first script; the answer decides whether this box's spread is the kernel's on every GPU or SM120's.

E1, the schedule (`e1_tp1_100`, `e1_tp2_100`: the matrix's tp=1 and tp=2 SP on cells with `--training.steps 100`): the debug config has `warmup_steps=2`, `decay_ratio=0.8`, linear decay to 0, so a 10-step run holds the peak learning rate for steps 3-4 and decays from step 5, while a 100-step run holds it until step 20. The 100-step tp=1 run is bitwise with the 10-step one for steps 1-4 and a different trajectory from step 5 (`8.06848` vs `8.049` at step 5), so the 10-step rows in the body are not the first ten of a 100-step run; 4500's protocol is the 100-step one, and the A100 tables should be 100-step tables. Within the 100-step runs, tp=2 vs tp=1 on this box: `+0.02%` at step 1, `+14.5%` at step 7, `-13.0%` at step 10, `+4.0%` at step 20, `+0.26%` at step 30, `-3.0%` at step 50, `-14.6%` at step 70, `+4.9%` at step 100. The schedule does not explain the gap to 4500's H100 table (`0.15%` at step 10, `2.7%` at step 100).

The per-step amplification scales with the learning rate: the spread is the optimizer's steps amplifying the per-layer floor the fused KDA kernel sets (E2), not a property of any one parallelism. Where that leaves the B200/H100 question: the schedule is not it (E1), the dynamics are the config's and would be the same there (E3), so the remaining candidates are the kernel's intermediate precision on those GPUs (E2 on the A100 decides) and whatever else differs in 4500's runs (their dp1 step-1 loss is `12.388` against `12.612` for the same head and seed here, so their data or init path is not this one). Until the A100 numbers exist, the honest statement is the body's: on this hardware the 10-step column measures the flavor's sensitivity.
