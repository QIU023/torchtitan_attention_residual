## Thread 6, `torchtitan/models/kimi_k3/model.py:242` (discussion_r4056308907)

--- PASTE BEGIN ---

Without PP, 2 blocks x 4 layers, three things are alive at the end of the forward.

The stack. Each block opening makes a new tensor by `torch.cat`, and the layers of that block hold a reference to it, so the total is N(N + 1) / 2 columns of [T, D] for N blocks, one storage each. A column is released when the last layer holding it has run its backward.

The partial sum of the open block, one [T, D] per layer, which is what a plain residual keeps. Under activation checkpointing it is recomputed rather than kept.

The residual read itself, twice per layer and once at the head. Each call upcasts the stack and the partial sum to fp32 and keeps two [T, N + 1, D] tensors until that layer's backward, 8 to 12 MiB per layer at T = 256 and D = 1024. This is the term that makes the saved set unlike a standard residual block, and `SelectiveAC`, the flavor's default, recomputes it.

Measured on that shape with `saved_tensors_hooks`, memory still allocated after the forward above the parameters: 383 MiB with no AC, 63 MiB with `SelectiveAC`, 15.5 MiB with full AC, of which the stack columns are 1.5 MiB in both AC modes.

One correction to the bot's summary: without AC the bf16 stack columns are not retained by the graph, since no op saves them; what is retained is their fp32 upcast inside each residual read. The three-column picture is the AC one, which is the default.

#4780, filed against main because the same function is the head's aggregation, replaces that body with an `autograd.Function` that saves references plus three fp32 vectors of [N + 1, T] per call and recomputes the upcasts in backward.
