Block Attention Residual under Pipeline Parallelism: one block, two gradient channels

P = pipeline degree, V = virtual stages per rank, T = P·V stages; stage S runs on rank S mod P (Interleaved1F1B). A block committed by stage S_p is fresh on the wire for P−1 hops, then lives in every rank's cache.

forward P2P (the delta) channel A: PP's own backward P2P (SEND_B) channel B: rank-local slot bridge read from the rank cache, no communication
  • A block committed by stage S_p is fresh on the wire for P−1 hops (S_p+1 .. S_p+P−1). From S_p+P on, every receiving rank already holds it: its previous virtual stage was S−P. The per-hop payload is therefore bounded by the commits of the last P−1 stages, independent of depth.
  • Which channel a consumer uses depends on one thing: whether the producer is on the consumer's rank. Same rank (S_c ≡ S_p mod P, S_c > S_p) takes channel B; every other consumer, fresh or relayed, takes channel A.
  • Channel B exists because a same-rank consumer must not walk into the producer's autograd graph: that frees it, and the producer's own backward then fails with "backward through the graph a second time". So the cache stores a detached copy and the gradient crosses by a slot instead.
  • Plain 1F1B is V = 1: channel B count is zero, the rank-shared cache is empty, and the transport falls back to shipping the whole carrier.
