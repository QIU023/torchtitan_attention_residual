One thing the tables above leave out: when a micro-batch's block activations are freed, taking the 2 blocks x 4 layers pp2 x vp2 example.

- Rank cache (forward): a block enters the rank's cache in the forward of the stage that brings it onto the rank (the commit, or the first receive), as a detached view of that stage's stack or received payload, not a copy: r0 puts `e` in at s0 and `x4` at s2, r1 puts both `e` (received) and `x4` (committed) in at s1. The rank drops all of a micro-batch's blocks right after the forward of its last stage (r0 at s2, r1 at s3), before any backward of that micro-batch.
- Backward: what the backward needs is not in the cache. Each stage keeps its own input stack (assembled from the payload and the cache) and its saved activations in the pipeline's `fwd_cache`, and frees them in its own backward of that micro-batch, the same as with the cache off. So a block's forward values on a rank are freed stage by stage as the backward goes s3 -> s0.
- Gradient deposits: a deposit lives from the backward of the stage that read the block from the cache to the backward of the stage that brought the block onto the rank, which adds it in and drops it: on r1, `de` and `dx4` from s3 to s1; on r0, `de` from s2 to s0. Each rank checks that no deposit is left after its first stage's backward.
