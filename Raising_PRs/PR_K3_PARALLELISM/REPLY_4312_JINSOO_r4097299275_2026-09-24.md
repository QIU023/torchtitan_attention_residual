Its P2P is batched per action (`_batch_p2p`, "Mixed batches still use batch_isend_irecv for deadlock avoidance"), recvs are placed by the sender's side ("This helps ensure a sane (non-hanging) ordering of sends and recvs"), deferred recvs keep "deadlock-safe ordering via rank-parity P2P ordering"; `_sorted_batch_p2p` exists "to avoid hangs in case of skip connections" but the looped runtime does not use it ("TODO: Does not use sorted_batch_isend_irecv(). - Skip sends would therefore be issued by the model outside the runtime, in forward and again for the gradients in backward, and ordered by hand against the schedule's batched P2P under 1F1B. Their data dependencies agree with the pipeline's order, but blocking waits and per-communicator FIFO order can still form a cycle; nothing in torch or torchtitan orders them today.

> it seems like you can combine option 1 and 2 to reduce the peak?

Yes: with sends that skip ranks, the peak halves at the same volume. This PR keeps every block on the stage-to-stage send the schedule already issues, so the transport adds no P2P op. Under that constraint $p-1$ is the floor at vp 1, since the last rank has no earlier stage to cache on and every earlier block arrives through its one incoming hop.

The skip sends would sit outside torch's pipelining runtime. It only issues P2P between adjacent stages, batched and ordered per action, and `PipelineScheduleMulti`, which the looped schedules subclass, raises on non-adjacent stage communication as "skip connections, which are unsupported in this schedule runtime". The model would have to issue those sends itself, and again for their gradients in backward, ordered by hand against the schedule's own P2P under 1F1B so that no rank waits on a peer that waits on it. torchtitan's pipeline code issues no P2P of its own today.

Per micro-batch, with one block per stage, stage $s$ on rank $s \bmod p$ and $p$ even (the sends repeat for the gradients in backward):

| | peak message (blocks) | blocks sent | P2P sends per direction |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| this PR, vp 1 | $p-1$ | $p(p-1)/2$ | $p-1$, the schedule's own |
| this PR, vp $v>1$ | $p-1$ | $p(p-1)(2v-1)/2$ | $pv-1$, the schedule's own |
| your arrangement, vp 1 | $p/2$ | $p(p-1)/2$ | $p-1$ plus $(p-2)^2/4$ skip sends |
| your arrangement, vp $v>1$ | $p/2$ | $p(p-1)(2v-1)/2$ | $pv-1$ plus $(p-2)(2pv-p-2)/4$ skip sends |

At pp8 that is 9 skip sends per micro-batch and direction at vp 1 and 81 at vp 4. With the released model's 8 blocks, the peak is 7 at pp8 x vp1, 3 at pp4 x vp2, 2 at pp8 x vp4 and 4 at pp16 x vp2, below $p-1$ once stages outnumber blocks. At vp 1 the cache gives nothing, so a hybrid transport for that case would be a follow-up of its own, once its ordering is shown deadlock free.

If the concern is a rank's memory rather than the message: the reading stage needs the whole stack resident either way, so a smaller message does not shrink it. Two drafts stacked on this PR address that: #4765 parks the blocks a rank caches for its later stages on pinned host memory between reads, and #4764 moves saved activations from the heavy PP ranks to a lighter one.

One cell of the table: the hop from rank 2 to rank 3 needs blocks 1 and 2; with 0 and 2, rank 3 gets block 0 twice and never block 1.
