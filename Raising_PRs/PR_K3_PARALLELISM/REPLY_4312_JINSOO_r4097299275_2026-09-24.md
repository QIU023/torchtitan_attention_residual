## Research behind the answer (not for pasting)

- No cache is missed and nothing is sent twice. On the PR's own tables (`BlockLayoutTables`, 93 layers, block 12, 8 blocks) the volume equals option 1's minimum in all six shapes of the 09-11 reply (28, 18, 7, 52, 24, 96), with zero duplicate deliveries; peaks 7, 3, 1, 2, 1, 4. `delta_to_send(s)` is everything committed so far minus what the receiving rank holds, i.e. the blocks since that rank's previous stage, which under adjacent-only transport can arrive through no earlier hop.
- His table follows a parity rule: block b rides the adjacent hops to offsets 1 and every even offset, and goes directly from its rank to offsets 3, 5, .... As written, the hop 2 to 3 carries {0, 2}: rank 3 then gets block 0 twice and never block 1; the rule gives {1, 2}.
- Closed forms, one block per stage, stage s on rank s mod p, p even, verified by `transport_model_2026-09-24.py` (this folder) for p in 2..16 and v in 1..8 (every block reaches every later rank exactly once in both schemes):
  - this PR: peak p-1 for every v; volume p(p-1)(2v-1)/2; the block traffic rides the pv-1 hops the schedule already sends, 0 added P2P ops.
  - his rule (generalised to v > 1 per round of p stages): peak p/2; the same volume; the same pv-1 hops plus (p-2)(2pv-p-2)/4 direct non-adjacent sends per direction, (p-2)^2/4 at v = 1 (p = 8: 9 at v 1, 33 at v 2, 81 at v 4, 177 at v 8; p = 16: 49 / 161 / 385 / 833).
  - at vp 1 this PR is option 2 exactly (no rank has an earlier stage to cache on), so the cache gives nothing there; its benefit is at vp > 1 (option 2's peak is pv-1).
- torch's pipelining runtime (read in `/workspace/venv_bfx9`, torch 2.15.0.dev20260906): stages send only to stage_index +/- 1 (`stage.py`, `get_fwd_send_ops` and the peers at L1225-1272); `PipelineScheduleMulti._validate_adjacent_stage_communication` (`schedules.py` L2050-2070) raises "PipelineScheduleMulti only supports adjacent-stage communication ... This commonly indicates skip connections, which are unsupported in this schedule runtime"; `_PipelineScheduleRuntime` subclasses it, and Interleaved1F1B, LoopedBFS, InterleavedZeroBubble, ZBVZeroBubble and DualPipeV subclass that. Its P2P is batched per action (`_batch_p2p`, "Mixed batches still use batch_isend_irecv for deadlock avoidance"), recvs are placed by the sender's side ("This helps ensure a sane (non-hanging) ordering of sends and recvs"), deferred recvs keep "deadlock-safe ordering via rank-parity P2P ordering"; `_sorted_batch_p2p` exists "to avoid hangs in case of skip connections" but the looped runtime does not use it ("TODO: Does not use sorted_batch_isend_irecv(). As a result, this schedule does not support models with skip connections").
- torchtitan issues no P2P of its own in pipeline code: `git grep` on upstream main `07b97cbfb` finds no `isend`, `irecv`, `batch_isend_irecv`, `P2POp`, `send_object_list` or `dist.send/recv` under `torchtitan/`; GraphPP's runner (`experiments/graph_trainer/graph_pp/runner.py`) only waits on the recv ops torch's `_PipelineScheduleRuntime` posts (`schedule.fwd_recv_ops`, `_wait_batch_p2p`). `rl/generator.py`'s send/recv are Monarch ports, not torch.distributed.

Their data dependencies agree with the pipeline's order, but blocking waits and per-communicator FIFO order can still form a cycle; nothing in torch or torchtitan orders them today.

On the PR head the message size still matters through torch's per-micro-batch receive buffers, which persist. That is the buffer effect K3_2P8T models, and `pp_review_optimize` removes it by receiving into the rank store on demand.

> it seems like you can combine option 1 and 2 to reduce the peak?

Yes: with sends that skip ranks, the peak drops from $p-1$ to $p/2$ blocks at the same volume. One cell of your table needs a fix: the hop from rank 2 to rank 3 carries blocks 1 and 2, since with 0 and 2 rank 3 never gets block 1. This PR keeps every block on the stage-to-stage sends the schedule already issues, so it adds no P2P op. Under that constraint $p-1$ is the floor at vp 1: every earlier block reaches the last rank through its one incoming hop.

The skip sends would sit outside torch's pipelining runtime. `PipelineScheduleMulti`, which the looped schedules subclass, raises on non-adjacent stage communication as "skip connections, which are unsupported in this schedule runtime". The model would issue them itself, again for the gradients in backward, and order them by hand against the schedule's batched P2P under 1F1B. torchtitan's pipeline code has no P2P of its own to build on.

Per micro-batch and direction, with one block per stage, stage $s$ on rank $s \bmod p$, $p$ even and vp $v$ ($v = 1$ is your table):

| | peak message (blocks) | blocks sent | P2P sends |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| this PR | $p-1$ | $p(p-1)(2v-1)/2$ | $pv-1$, the schedule's own |
| with skip sends | $p/2$ | $p(p-1)(2v-1)/2$ | $pv-1$ plus $(p-2)(2pv-p-2)/4$ |

At pp8 that is 9 skip sends at vp 1 and 81 at vp 4. With the released model's 8 blocks, this PR's peak is already 2 at pp8 x vp4 and 4 at pp16 x vp2, since stages outnumber blocks. Either way the reading stage assembles the whole stack, so the stack a rank holds is the same. For a rank's memory, the drafts stacked on this PR move saved activations off the device: #4765 to pinned host memory and #4764 to a lighter PP rank over Mooncake, as the K3 report describes. Happy to file the hybrid transport as a separate issue; its sends first need an order against the schedule's P2P that is shown deadlock free.
