## The three high-level comments

**"Too many install / patch / hook / thread."** Agreed, and they are gone: the adapter (1228 lines wrapping `forward_one_chunk` / `backward_one_chunk` / `step`, a thread-local micro-batch id, a tensor grad hook and an autograd Function for the same-rank gradient path) is replaced by `AttnResPipelineStage`, a `PipelineStage` subclass of about 390 lines that overrides `forward_one_chunk`, `backward_one_chunk`, `_retrieve_recv_grads`, `_compute_outputs` and `_compute_input_grads`, reached through one generic hook, `pipeline_llm(..., stage_class=...)`. The stage assembles the block stack from its rank's store plus the received delta, the model takes and returns the whole stack, and the backward hands the received columns back over the wire and deposits the stored ones for the stage that brought the block onto the rank. No patch, no thread-local, no hook. The numerics match the adapter to within one bf16 rounding (per-parameter gradients at step 1: median 3.5e-5, max 9.2e-3), and step 1 is bit-identical to dp1 on every pp x vp cell.

**"If skip connections across stages become common, build general infra in `torch.distributed.pipelining`."** Agreed. What the subclass needs from the library, in order of value: stage outputs with more than one consumer (a routing table the library could own; `BlockLayoutTables` is that table computed outside it), rank-local delivery for a consumer on the producer's rank with the gradient merged by the schedule (the deposit-and-collect the subclass does), and dense gradient receive buffers (`_create_grad_recv_info` sizes them from the strides of the next stage's input gradients, which are views when the stage's first op on the input is a `cat`; that surfaced as "Tensors for P2P must be non-overlapping and dense" on the 32-stage cell). With those three in `PipelineStage`, the subclass reduces to declaring which outputs are blocks. Happy to open the upstream issue for the buffer sizing.

**"The numerics feel wrong."** The step-10 spread came from two effects that are not the transport: `get_total_norm` reduces the per-tensor norms in the gradients' dtype and the pipeline groups the parameters differently per topology, so the bf16 clip coefficient moves per topology (pytorch PR 194033 carries the reduction in fp32); and FlexAttention's autotune picks kernels by benchmark timing that `--debug.deterministic` does not control. The evidence that survives both is at step 1: the loss is bit-identical to dp1 in every cell of the 30-layer irregular debug model (two to thirty-two stages, uneven splits, delta transport and whole-stack transport), and the per-parameter gradients differ from dp1 at the level bf16 summation order produces (median 1.5e-4, max 1.6e-2 on 16-element vectors), with no parameter group standing out. The body now carries two matrices, the fp32-norm one first, each with one line saying what it shows.

## Round 2 (2026-09-04): the questions that get an answer rather than a change

**`stage_class` on `pipeline_llm`: what it is for, and is the shape right.** It is the one seam that lets a model run its stages on a `PipelineStage` subclass: `pipeline_kimi_k3` calls `pipeline_llm(model, stage_class=AttnResPipelineStage, ...)`. There is no separate multi-stage class to swap in: under `Interleaved1F1B` every virtual stage is its own `PipelineStage` and the schedule loops over them, so the constructor in `_pipeline_module_split` is the only place the class is named, and a parameter with the plain class as its default (`type[PipelineStage] = PipelineStage`, next to the optional `get_mesh` the same function already takes) leaves every other model untouched. The alternative, a model-side pipelining function that builds its stages without `pipeline_llm`, copies all of `_pipeline_module_split`; the +10/-2 seam is the smaller change.

**The 93-layer shape and the uneven split.** `kimi_k3_full_attention_layers` is not there to run 93 layers on this box (the released shape does not fit eight 16 GB GPUs by an order of magnitude); it is the deduction asked for on `__init__.py:460`, "(3 KDA + 1 MLA) * k + remainder", so the 30-layer debug model and the 93-layer model share one rule. pp8 x vp4 on 93 layers is 95 units over 32 stages, thirty-one stages of three units and one of two, and that works by construction: the change that allows an uneven split is `2b6d38018`. The old adapter simulated an equal split offline and gated on it, because under `Interleaved1F1B` a rank sees only its own stages and had no global layer-to-stage map; now every rank all-gathers its local map once over the pipeline group, `BlockLayoutTables` is built from the split the trainer actually applied, and the stage routes by the schedule's own `stage_index_to_group_rank`, so any schedule and any contiguous split work; a 5 / 7 / 6 / 6 split is a unit test, and the 30-layer matrix runs uneven splits from 2 to 32 stages.

**What moving the whole Block AttnRes pipeline into `torch.distributed.pipelining` would package.** Every other model runs the standard path, `pipeline_llm` onto `PipelineStage` plus the stock schedules; only the attention residual needs more, and the schedule needs no change at all since every gradient rides the existing `SEND_B`. In the order the library would absorb them: the five overridden points of `AttnResPipelineStage` become an extension surface (`forward_one_chunk` and `backward_one_chunk` are public, `_retrieve_recv_grads`, `_compute_outputs` and `_compute_input_grads` would need to become documented API), which is the same as three generic capabilities, stage outputs with more than one consumer (`act_send_info` / `args_recv_info` from a chain to a DAG), rank-local delivery for a consumer on the producer's rank, and a chunk-id / lifecycle context; `RankStore`, the rank-level cache shared by the virtual stages and released per micro-batch, becomes a library object whose release point the schedule already knows (the rank's last stage for that micro-batch); `BlockLayoutTables` generalises to "output k of stage S is consumed by stages S+1..N" plus `stage_index_to_group_rank`, producing the per-hop tables, with nothing AttnRes-specific in it; the delta-or-whole transport is a switch on the stage. What stays with the model is the carrier convention: it takes and returns the whole stack, commits append at the end, and a block's first layer joins the stack before attending, so the library routes an append-only segmented payload and never learns what a block residual is.

**Is the step-10 spread a numerical problem (draft, the census and the 100-step curves fill the two placeholders).** No numerical problem that the evidence can find: step 1 is bit-identical across all cells (loss 12.51030), the float32 total gradient norms agree to 2e-4 (dp1 16.1631, pp2 x vp4 16.1661, pp4 x vp4 16.1646, pp8 x vp4 16.1656), and the step-1 per-parameter gradient comparison finds no parameter group off (median relative difference 1.5e-4 over 918 parameters). The step-10 spread is the trajectory's sensitivity in this debug setup, not the transport: the same dp1 cell moves by 3.4% at step 10 (3.49625 to 3.37903) when only the grad-norm reduction precision changes, dp1 vs dp2 moves by 6% in the CP matrix, and the PP cells land on both sides of dp1 (pp2 and pp8 below, pp4 above). The mechanism is Adam's first step: the update is $lr \cdot \mathrm{sign}(g)$ per element, so the elements whose gradient sits below bf16 rounding noise flip sign between any two runs that sum in a different order, and each flipped element moves by $2 \cdot lr$ the wrong way; the debug flavor is bf16 end to end (parameters and optimizer states), lr is 8e-4 with 2 warm-up steps, and the loss falls from 12.5 to 3.4 in ten steps, which amplifies that first-step difference. Measured over all 1.3B gradient elements at step 1: dp1 vs pp2 x vp4 flips the sign of 0.223% of them and dp1 vs pp8 x vp4 0.227% (84% of those sit below a hundredth of their tensor's rms; two dp1 runs on fresh compile caches flip none and are bitwise), a 9.5% relative difference of the first Adam update from gradients whose per-parameter norms agree to 2e-4; element-wise the gradients differ by about 1.1% in every parameter group alike, and 32 stages flip no more than 8. Over 100 steps at the same seed the curves of dp1, pp2 x vp4, pp8 x vp4 and the whole-stack transport <curves: mean loss over steps 51 to 100 per cell and the band they cross in>. The float32 grad-norm rows do not remove the spread (the five cells spread 6.2% at step 10 under the float32 norm, 5.6% under bf16), so the body no longer credits the bf16 total-norm grouping with it; the full per-step data is in the logbook (`PP_STEP10_SPREAD_2026-09-04.md`).
