## The three high-level comments

**"Too many install / patch / hook / thread."** Agreed, and they are gone: the adapter (1228 lines wrapping `forward_one_chunk` / `backward_one_chunk` / `step`, a thread-local micro-batch id, a tensor grad hook and an autograd Function for the same-rank gradient path) is replaced by `AttnResPipelineStage`, a `PipelineStage` subclass of about 390 lines that overrides `forward_one_chunk`, `backward_one_chunk`, `_retrieve_recv_grads`, `_compute_outputs` and `_compute_input_grads`, reached through one generic hook, `pipeline_llm(..., stage_class=...)`. The stage assembles the block stack from its rank's store plus the received delta, the model takes and returns the whole stack, and the backward hands the received columns back over the wire and deposits the stored ones for the stage that brought the block onto the rank. No patch, no thread-local, no hook. The numerics match the adapter to within one bf16 rounding (per-parameter gradients at step 1: median 3.5e-5, max 9.2e-3), and step 1 is bit-identical to dp1 on every pp x vp cell.

**"If skip connections across stages become common, build general infra in `torch.distributed.pipelining`."** Agreed. What the subclass needs from the library, in order of value: stage outputs with more than one consumer (a routing table the library could own; `BlockLayoutTables` is that table computed outside it), rank-local delivery for a consumer on the producer's rank with the gradient merged by the schedule (the deposit-and-collect the subclass does), and dense gradient receive buffers (`_create_grad_recv_info` sizes them from the strides of the next stage's input gradients, which are views when the stage's first op on the input is a `cat`; that surfaced as "Tensors for P2P must be non-overlapping and dense" on the 32-stage cell). With those three in `PipelineStage`, the subclass reduces to declaring which outputs are blocks. Happy to open the upstream issue for the buffer sizing.

**"The numerics feel wrong."** The step-10 spread came from two effects that are not the transport: `get_total_norm` reduces the per-tensor norms in the gradients' dtype and the pipeline groups the parameters differently per topology, so the bf16 clip coefficient moves per topology (pytorch PR 194033 carries the reduction in fp32); and FlexAttention's autotune picks kernels by benchmark timing that `--debug.deterministic` does not control. The evidence that survives both is at step 1: the loss is bit-identical to dp1 in every cell of the 30-layer irregular debug model (two to thirty-two stages, uneven splits, delta transport and whole-stack transport), and the per-parameter gradients differ from dp1 at the level bf16 summation order produces (median 1.5e-4, max 1.6e-2 on 16-element vectors), with no parameter group standing out. The body now carries two matrices, the fp32-norm one first, each with one line saying what it shows.
