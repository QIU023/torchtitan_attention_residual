## Summary

In torchtitan's vision-language models the vision encoder repeats the same work on every CP rank, sits on the first pipeline stage, and takes whatever media load each data-parallel rank's samples carry; this RFC proposes one model-generic layer that parallelizes the encoder across the parallelism dimensions, starting with the two techniques the Kimi K2.5 and K3 reports describe (dynamic CP of the encoder and the decoupled encoder process, DEP), and asks for agreement on the interface and on how to evaluate the gains before the code leaves the Kimi K3 folder.

- Every vision-language model in torchtitan builds its encoder from the common `VisionTransformerBlock` and `VisionAttention`: Kimi K2.5, Kimi K3, Qwen3.5, Muse Spark and Muse Glimmer.
- #4380 (dynamic CP) and #4381 (DEP) implement the two techniques for Kimi K3 only, in `kimi_k3/vision_cp/` and `kimi_k3/pipeline_parallel/vision_dep/`.
- The proposal: an encoder contract in `models/common`, and opt-in wrappers that apply each technique to any encoder that meets it.

## The vision encoder under each parallelism dimension on main

| Dimension | What main does with the encoder | Cost | Known technique | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DP / FSDP | Each DP rank encodes its own samples; FSDP shards the encoder over dp_shard. Packing fills micro-batches by sequence length after DP sharding (`MMSamplePackingConfig`, and the `TODO(data-global-pack-plan)` in `mm_datasets.py`). | Ranks with more patches straggle. | Balance media work across DP ranks when packing (that TODO; DistTrain's data reordering). | Open |
| TP | The common vision sharding configs shard the encoder's linears over TP; every TP rank runs every patch. | Small GEMMs plus TP collectives for a model much narrower than the decoder. | A smaller TP degree for the encoder (Megatron-Core's encoder TP for LLaVA), or a replicated encoder with items split over the TP ranks. | Open question |
| CP | Every CP rank encodes the full vision input (`multimodal_input_sharding`); since #5266 the encoder is CP-invariant. | Encoder time and peak memory do not shrink with CP; one large image or long video dominates. | Dynamic CP (Kimi K3 report, sec 5.2.3). | #4380, Kimi K3 only |
| PP | The encoder sits on the first stage (`pipeline_first_stage_module_fqns`); text-only micro-batches run a dummy vision forward. | Stage 0's time and memory swing with the images in each micro-batch. | DEP (Kimi K2.5), with the encoder work placed in pipeline bubbles (Kimi K3 report, sec 5.2.3; Optimus). | #4381, Kimi K3 only |
| EP | The encoders are dense and run on the data dimensions EP is carved from. | Nothing specific to the encoder. | None needed. | No change |

## The two known techniques

- Dynamic CP (Kimi K3 report, sec 5.2.3):
  - "A single large image is partitioned along the patch dimension across multiple devices", and attention gathers keys and values across the CP ranks.
  - CP groups split into sub-CP groups, and several large images are distributed over them "in a load-balanced manner".
  - #4380: images with at least `dynamic_cp_min_patches` patches split by rows of merge blocks over a sub-CP group, smaller images stay whole, and one all-gather returns the vision bank; with the option off, main's path is unchanged.
- DEP (Kimi K2.5 report; Kimi K3 report, sec 5.2.3):
  - K2.5: every GPU holds the encoder, the vision forward of the whole batch runs first, balanced by image or patch counts, and the encoder is recomputed and backpropagated after the backbone; the report gives 90% of text-only training efficiency for multimodal training.
  - K3: "The ViT forward passes of the first PP micro-batches are executed synchronously upfront, the remaining forward passes are scheduled into pipeline bubbles, and the backward passes are handled analogously."
  - #4381: an encoder copy on every pipeline rank, refreshed from stage 0 each step; features and gradients cross ranks on a process group per pipeline group; with `bubble=True` the work follows Figure 11 of the K3 report; the copies' gradients reduce into stage 0's encoder in fp32.

### Measured so far (Kimi K3 only)

The encoder alone (Kimi K3's: 27 layers, dim 1024, random bf16 weights), forward and backward over one micro-batch's images on H100, main `e61df55b6` against #4380 at `a2a97b88a`, the recipe's selective activation remat. Main encodes every image on every CP rank, so it measures the same at CP 1, 2 and 4. Cells are peak memory per GPU / time:

| Images (patches) | main | dynamic CP, CP=2 | dynamic CP, CP=4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| one 4032 px image (82944) | 55.2 GiB / 10.9 s | 28.5 GiB / 5.6 s | 15.3 GiB / 2.9 s |
| four 2016 px images (82944) | 55.2 GiB / 3.1 s | 28.6 GiB / 1.6 s | 15.3 GiB / 0.80 s |
| 16 frames of 448 px in 4-frame items (16384) | 11.6 GiB / 0.22 s | 6.3 GiB / 0.14 s | 3.6 GiB / 0.13 s |

- With dynamic CP off, #4380 trains bitwise to main for 100 steps; the numerics with it on are in #4380.
- DEP's effect on step time has not been measured on current main yet. Its plan tests check the Figure 11 layout and the bound that, with one equal image per micro-batch, at most `1 - p/M` of the encoder work fits in bubbles for p pipeline ranks and M micro-batches.
- The gain depends on the encoder's share of the step. A FLOP estimate for Kimi K3's released shapes puts the encoder (forward, recompute and backward) at 0.2% to 17% of a training step, from 448 px images at 10% vision tokens to 3584 px images at 90%. On the debug flavors the share is a few percent, too small to show any gain.

## Proposal

- An encoder contract in `models/common/vision_encoder.py`, which the encoders above already nearly meet:
  - `forward(pixel_values, *, grid_thw)` over packed items, returning the bank rows in item order (all five already take this signature);
  - the output rows per item, and the patch rows one output row spans (the merge kernel);
  - for dynamic CP only, the position embeddings and the merge for a band of rows of one item.
- The vision path: the modules between `pixel_values` and the bank (the encoder alone for Kimi and Qwen3.5; the encoder, adapter and projection for the Muse models), declared on `MultimodalModel` next to `multimodal_encoder_fqns`.
- Dynamic CP as a wrapper:
  - `apply_dynamic_cp(encoder, ...)` swaps the encoder's class for a CP subclass the way `fully_shard` does, so FQNs and checkpoints are unchanged; the attention's KV gather goes on the common `VisionAttention`.
  - The planner (`vision_cp/plan.py`) and the gather move to common unchanged; the per-model part is the band positions and merge from the contract.
  - It reuses #4380's change to `MultimodalModel`: with dynamic CP on, the encoder is replicated rather than invariant on CP, and FSDP shards it over dp_shard x cp.
- DEP as a wrapper:
  - `MultimodalModel.pipeline()` builds the stages as today, then swaps each stage's class for a DEP subclass and wraps the schedule's `step`.
  - The vision path is replicated on every pipeline rank, and `MultimodalModel.forward` takes precomputed vision embeddings.
  - The plan, runtime, stage and schedule move from `kimi_k3/pipeline_parallel/vision_dep/` unchanged; Kimi K3 keeps its AttnRes stage by class composition, as #4381 does today.
  - The placement needs only a cost per micro-batch, so the same wrapper could also serve non-vision encoders such as Muse Spark's audio encoder.
- Configuration: one opt-in config per encoder on `MultimodalModel.Config` (the dynamic CP threshold; DEP on or off; bubble placement on or off, with its cost ratio). Everything is off by default, so main's paths do not change.

## Evaluation plan

- Baseline: main with the same model, shapes and data; every comparison on one warmed compile cache.
- Metrics: wall-clock step time and tokens per second per GPU (not MFU, see #4009); peak memory on every rank; under PP, each stage's busy time; the encoder's share of the step, measured with DEP off at zero and at full image load.
- Numerics: with the feature off, bitwise to main; with it on, step-1 loss and text gradients bitwise, encoder gradients within one bf16 unit counted per tensor, and a 100-step loss curve.
- Workloads: images at 2K to 4K px, video at 16 to 64 frames, a realistic packed mix with text-only samples, and a text-heavy control where no gain is expected.
- Shapes: CP 2, 4 and 8; PP 4 and 8 with 2 and 4 virtual stages and M from 2p to 4p; one node of H100 or B200.
- Models: Kimi K3 and Qwen3.5, so the result is not one model's.

## Questions for the maintainers

- Placement: the contract and dynamic CP in `models/common`, DEP in `distributed/pipeline_parallel`, or one multimodal package?
- PP seam: is a stage class swap plus a wrapped `step` acceptable, or should DEP be a schedule subclass in core?
- Order: land #4380 and #4381 as the Kimi K3 reference and move them afterwards, or go straight to the common layer?
- Evaluation: which workloads, hardware and step-time threshold decide adoption?
- DP balancing: does the `data-global-pack-plan` TODO have an owner this should feed into?
- TP: is there interest in running small encoders at a lower TP degree than the decoder?
