### Parallelism matrix on the eager reference's own debug config

Multimodal (MoonViT + backbone), seed 42, deterministic, gradient accumulation on. Thirteen degree-2 configurations -- single-GPU, `fsdp2`, `pp2`, `cp2`, `tp2`, every 3-of-4 combination of FSDP/TP/PP/CP, all repeated with EP -- plus max-degree cells on 8 GPUs:

| | eager | compiled |
|---|---|---|
| 3 / 10 / 100 steps | 13/13 | 10/13 (3 steps) |
| 100 steps, report architecture (final layer global) | 13/13 | 10/13 (3 steps) |

All eighteen cells were also run compiled on the report architecture: **15/18** as-is, **18/18** with the shim below. Eager on that architecture is 18/18 at 10 steps.

| max-degree cell | result |
|---|---|
| `ep8_fsdp8` (1 expert/rank) | pass, eager and compiled |
| `pp4` (uneven 13-layer split, vision tower on stage 0) | pass |
| `tp4`, `cp4` (1 head/rank) | pass |
| `pp8` | pass, after [one fix](https://github.com/QIU023/torchtitan_attention_residual/blob/main/phase13_k3like_48b_posttrain/TWIN_MATRIX_2026-08-04.md#the-pp8-defect-cause-and-two-wrong-turns) (dense-grad on PP inputs) |

**18 of 18 attempted, on both model configurations.** Everything above now starts from a **shared step-0 checkpoint**, one per flavor, written with `--checkpoint.create_seed_checkpoint` and loaded by every cell -- suggested on the eager PR, and it changed the result qualitatively. Verified the load happens rather than assumed: cold start gives step-1 12.07003, the seeded run 12.09025.

It also exposed a defect a cold-start matrix structurally cannot see: **multimodal + PP could not load any checkpoint**. Under PP only the stage owning `embed_tokens` is re-wrapped as the multimodal model, so it names parameters `language_model.*` while other stages name them bare, and a non-PP save uses the prefixed form throughout. Six of eighteen cells failed on the first read. Not just seed checkpoints -- ordinary resume was broken identically, and every matrix before this one cold-started, so 18/18 was achievable with this broken the whole time. Fixed with state-dict hooks on non-first stages.

What fixing init bought is a spread that **decomposes**. Seven cells with no TP and no CP -- `fsdp2`, `pp2`, `pp4`, `pp8`, `ep2 x fsdp2`, `ep8 x fsdp8` and single-GPU -- agree **bit-for-bit** at step 1 (12.07418). FSDP, pipeline parallelism at degrees 2/4/8 and expert parallelism at degrees 2/8 introduce no numerical difference at all; the cells that differ are exactly those where TP or CP changes reduction order, clustering at 12.07565 (CP), 12.07566 (TP), 12.07590 (both). Cold start had 0.041 of spread with init and parallelism superimposed and no way to separate them; this has 0.009, all accounted for.

**That agreement does not persist across the run**, and we do not have an explanation for why. Spread grows to 10.3% by step 50 and 11.9% by step 100, including among the seven cells that were bit-identical at step 1 (0% -> 3.3% -> 11.9%).

A first version of this section attributed that to MoE routing -- top-k being a discrete choice, so any floating-point difference eventually flips which expert a token reaches. **A dense control refutes it.** The same thirteen non-EP cells on the same architecture with `first_k_dense_replace` set to the layer count, so every layer is a plain FFN and nothing else changes, diverge *more* in relative terms, not less: 13.6% at step 50 and 24.5% at step 100 against MoE's 9.5% and 12.0%. In absolute terms the two are the same order (dense 0.029 at step 100, MoE 0.043) and the relative gap is mostly that dense converges three times further, so the same absolute difference is a larger fraction of a smaller loss.

So trajectory divergence under differing reduction order is generic to training here, not a property of routing, and the earlier attribution was a category error: our measured MoE-versus-dense result (cross-layout gradient direction 1-cos = 7.7e-1 on MoE against 1.06e-4 on dense) is about per-parameter gradients at a single step from shared weights, which is a different quantity from loss-trajectory separation over a hundred steps. Step-1 identity is the operation-level equivalence claim and it is exact; the later horizons show that every configuration trains stably to a comparable loss, and nothing stronger.

Full per-cell tables for all three matrices are in [the seed-checkpoint record](https://github.com/QIU023/torchtitan_attention_residual/blob/main/phase13_k3like_48b_posttrain/SEED_MATRIX_2026-08-04.md).

- **The compiled gaps are one upstream operator limitation, not ours**: `torch._grouped_mm` rejects a zero contraction dimension -- the weight-gradient shape a rank sees when it ends up with zero routed tokens in total, which the ep2 x TP/PP/CP combinations produce (per-group empties alone do not: `ep8_fsdp8` passes compiled unshimmed). Five-line reproduction, no model/compile/distributed, being filed against pytorch/pytorch; with a local [shim](https://github.com/QIU023/torchtitan_attention_residual/blob/main/phase13_k3like_48b_posttrain/matrix_scripts/gmm_shim.py) that re-strides exactly as the proposed patch would, every affected cell passes -- **the shim is ours, so the unshimmed numbers stay the reported ones**.
- **PP depth beyond the matrix**: `PP8xVP4` (32 layers, one layer per virtual stage, Interleaved1F1B) runs clean on our own flavor at `seq_len 1024`; the 8192 configuration the pressure test was built for exceeds this box and is pending bigger hardware. [Details](https://github.com/QIU023/torchtitan_attention_residual/blob/main/phase13_k3like_48b_posttrain/TWIN_MATRIX_2026-08-04.md#pp8xvp4-after-the-change).
- **A shared-code gradient fix fell out of the matrix**: TP-without-EP legs under-reduced gradients below the routed experts (3.10x whole-model grad_norm gap, invisible in loss -- Adam is scale-invariant); root cause in the shared MoE sharding config, reproduces on unmodified deepseek_v3, filed as #4054.
- **The released 2.8T config was audited against report Table 1** on a meta device: 2.779T total vs 2.78T, 105.8B activated vs 104.2B, layer composition / latent / experts / vision tower matching field for field; the MTP layer is in the report but shipped disabled (`num_nextn_predict_layers: 0`), and enabling it costs ~30B that Table 1's total does not appear to include -- [scale audit](https://github.com/QIU023/torchtitan_attention_residual/blob/main/phase13_k3like_48b_posttrain/SCALE_AUDIT_2p8t_2026-08-04.md).

Caveats, briefly: the 100-step runs overfit a smoke dataset (stability evidence, not convergence -- real pretraining runs are queued); the primary matrix runs the eager reference's architecture deliberately (its final layer is KDA where the report and the released config end global -- raised on that PR as a config question; the report-architecture rows above cover the corrected layout); cross-stack numerical alignment is now blocked on our state-dict adapter round-tripping against theirs rather than on the rebase -- fixing init removed the RNG obstacle, but the same weights still have to land in both stacks, so these tables show eighteen configurations of one stack, not two stacks agreeing. Environment: torch `2.14.0.dev20260802+cu130`, fla-core `0.5.1`, 8x RTX 5060 Ti -- which cells can run is hardware-dependent (details in the matrix records), the losses are not. `PP8xVP4` at seq 8192 and real-data long runs are pending bigger hardware and will be posted then, not implied now.

### The interfaces the PP and EP slices touch, and who adds them: we do

Nothing to merge yet and nothing we are asking anyone to build. **PP (slot C)**: the eager decoder loop already threads `(h_BLD, block_residual_TND)` through every layer; the two missing seams -- an optional initial-stack argument and a layer-range slice, returning the pair -- land as our PP PR's first, separately reviewable commit. **EP (slot B)**: the per-expert <-> grouped-GEMM conversion (bidirectional key map with sliced-tensor indexing, `...experts.3.w1.weight <-> w1_EFD[3]`, plus the grouped experts module) already exists on the branch and ships with that PR, composing with the eager per-expert adapter rather than replacing it. TP and CP need no seam and rebase cleanly.
